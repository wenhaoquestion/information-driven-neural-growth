# Phase II scientific status

This publication copy reports historical September 2026 results, not training or stochastic reruns performed for publication. Authorship, portable paths and internal execution metadata were edited; the scientific conclusions were preserved. The manuscripts have not undergone external peer review.

Supported theory: an explicit tanh task pair has identical passive activation/label/residual laws but different immediately useful split directions; full input--residual context permits descent with matching two-task sample order. A separate even-parity construction separates fixed positive child proportions from a positive normalized singular path `p(t) ~ t^(d-4)` at the same two-child width, fixed intercept and conserved weighted incoming mean. This is an oracle existence construction, not learned growth. SSD and general boundary-weight escape are established prior work.

Historical empirical evidence contains 1,280 bridge configurations, two 96-configuration information-score ensembles, preserved pilots, 66 high-precision population cases and 20,000 score-sampling trials. At the designed four-state start, spectral versus random mean log loss is about 0.656061 versus 0.668094 after 30 updates, but about 0.649278 versus 0.649280 after 300. Ordinary pretraining/random starts weaken the benefit. Fresh operation-aligned binned CMI has no resolved advantage in the three tested tasks; the localized entropy proxy's favorable result is retained. Forced no-signal growth and overfitting are adverse findings, not excluded runs.

The holdout gate is heuristic and consumes extra labels and a continuation fit. The high-order path has tiny absolute gain and a saved d=10, t=0.3 failure. No general information-based trigger, learned singular path, repeated adaptation, deep real-data utility or architecture optimality is established. Later phases address different parts of these gaps; they do not retroactively change this one-event study.
