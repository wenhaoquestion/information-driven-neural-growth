\begin{abstract}
This working paper establishes two mathematical boundaries for a project
on information, retained summaries, and neural growth. First, exact
universal empirical residual queries for centered quadratic predictors
are equivalent to a finite collection of label, second-order, and
fourth-order moments. With $p=\binom{d+1}{2}$,
$q=\binom{d+3}{4}$, and fixed unweighted sample size $n\ge p+q$,
the minimum dimension of a continuous real-valued summary is exactly
$2p+q$, or $p+q$ additional coordinates when the label moment is
already supplied. At $d=12$ these counts are 1,521 and 1,443.
The result concerns every dataset and every query; it is not an
approximate-memory, finite-word, or generalization lower bound.
Second, a single smooth strictly convex binary generator with finite
endpoint slopes has unbounded optimal hierarchy price in excess
Bayes risk, even with four equally likely source states. Along the
constructed family indexed by $j$, deterministic and stochastic prices
are asymptotic to $j/2$ and $j/4$, respectively. The corresponding
total-risk prices tend to one.
A classical covariance-sketch guarantee gives an explicit approximate
query baseline. An appendix separates representation information,
forgetting, Bayes value, and fitted risk, including a zero-output
neural birth that gains representation information while preserving
prediction risk. Classical ingredients and prior project results
are identified; no priority or general learning guarantee is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Questions, context, and the limits of the claims}
\label{sec:introduction}

The preceding project studied finite representation hierarchies,
including logarithmic-loss obstructions and curvature comparisons
\cite{finiteproject}, and growth in a trainable quadratic network.
Its Phase~6 experiment asked
whether a retained historical moment improves final risk under a
declared work budget, rather than merely supplying a promising birth
direction~\cite{phase6}. That experiment and the earlier theory notes
already distinguished population moment formulas from exact empirical
residuals~\cite{phase5,phase6}. The present paper addresses two
mathematical questions left separate from those empirical comparisons:
what must a summary retain to answer every empirical residual query,
and whether finite endpoint slopes suffice to prevent an unbounded
excess-risk price of a compatible hierarchy.

For observations $(x_i,y_i)$, put $H_i=x_ix_i^\top-I_d$ and let
the centered quadratic predictor be
$q_B(x)+c=\langle B,xx^\top-I_d\rangle+c$.
Phase~6 proposes directions using $S_y-B$, where
$S_y=(2n)^{-1}\sum_i y_iH_i$. The exact empirical matrix residual is
instead
\begin{equation}
 G(B,c)=\frac1{2n}\sum_i\{y_i-\langle B,H_i\rangle-c\}H_i
 =S_y-T(B)-\frac c2\overline H,
 \qquad T(B)=\frac1{2n}\sum_i\langle B,H_i\rangle H_i.
 \label{eq:intro-residual}
\end{equation}
For standard Gaussian input and fixed sample-independent $B$,
$\E T(B)=B$ and $\E\overline H=0$. Replacing empirical quantities
by those population identities does not reproduce a realized query,
especially after $B$ has been chosen from the same history. The
memory result characterizes the stronger requirement of exact
answers for \emph{all} $B,c$ on \emph{all} datasets. It neither
claims that the implemented population-based proposal ought to be
exact nor assigns its empirical performance to a missing moment.

The two principal results have different domains and should not be
combined into an assertion that neural growth must fail or succeed.
The first is a representation theorem for empirical computations.
The second is an existence theorem for optimal nested partitions
under one fixed proper-loss generator. Neither theorem describes
finite-budget training of neural parameters. Table~\ref{tab:scope}
records the assumptions needed to read the conclusions.

The main tools have established antecedents. Empirical residuals are
normal-equation computations, finite polynomial moments are classical
representations, and invariance of domain gives dimensional
obstructions~\cite{mit,tchakaloff,tao}. Continuity restrictions and
pathological single-real encodings already play an explicit role in
the theory of set representations~\cite{wagstaff}. The finite
hierarchy problem uses Bregman centroid identities and the established
proper-score representation~\cite{banerjee2005,telgarsky2012,p7gneiting2007}.
An upper bound on a generator's first derivative is substantially
weaker than a uniform positive lower curvature bound or a global
quadratic comparison~\cite{ackermann2009}. We use those distinctions
to state a boundary result, not a complete classification or a claim
of first discovery.

\section{Interpretation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:discussion}

The exact memory theorem identifies what is lost when a historical
summary preserves only a label--quadratic cross moment. It also
identifies the assumptions that make a dimension lower bound valid.
The encoder must be continuous for the continuous theorem, and every
data-dependent state available to the decoder counts toward its
dimension. The linear-measurement version uses arbitrary weighted
measures or affine decoding; it must not be transferred to arbitrary
discontinuous additive encoders on equal-weight samples. The local
finite-bit bound uses a stated error norm and a nonquantified local
radius. None of these exact or high-accuracy results establishes
$\Omega(d^4)$ practical storage at a fixed useful tolerance.

The sketch corollary makes the approximation boundary constructive:
a covariance approximation of lifted quadratic features supplies
simultaneously valid approximate residuals for bounded query matrices.
Whether this is useful depends on the lifted matrix's spectral tail,
the desired accuracy, and the implementation cost. It remains
separate from estimation of population risk, selection of a birth
direction, and optimization of the enlarged network. No new training
experiment or sketch implementation accompanies this theoretical
increment.

The smooth-generator construction shows that a loss can have bounded
values, finite slopes, and interior strict convexity while its excess
hierarchy price is unbounded. The critical qualification is the
absence of a uniform positive curvature lower bound. Its source
weights need not become rare: all four remain $1/4$. Instead, the
generator contains increasingly weak curvature patterns at smaller
scales. The absolute distortions
vanish, and the common Bayes baseline dominates the total risk.
Thus an unbounded excess ratio need not describe a large absolute
prediction penalty or an unbounded total-risk ratio.

Appendix~\ref{sec:p7-information-boundaries} addresses a third,
supporting distinction. Even for logarithmic loss, a change of
representation has a forgetting term unless the representations
form a refinement. Information gain refers to an unrestricted
Bayes decoder; an unchanged output function has unchanged realized
risk. A zero-output birth can nevertheless add informative hidden
features. Finite optimization, decoder restriction, and population
estimation require their own analysis. These classical identities
do not convert either principal result into an autonomous-growth
principle.

The artifact preserves the research drafts, the imported manuscript
sections, source citations, independent review notes, and exact or
high-precision algebraic checks. The memory checks reconstruct
queries and explicit collisions and verify small-dimensional ranks
using rational arithmetic. Finite-cost enumeration checks the local
partition calculations; it does not replace the smooth-gluing proof
or prove an infinite asymptotic limit numerically. The build script
