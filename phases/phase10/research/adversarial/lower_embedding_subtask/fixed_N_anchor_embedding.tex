\section{Fixed-count embedding of the endpoint quartet}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The Phase 9 endpoint lower witness extends to every fixed number of
states $N\ge4$ at capacities $(N-2,N-1)$, without increasing curvature
degree. The deterministic and stochastic lower constants can be the
original $1/(32d_n)$ and $1/(64d_n)$, respectively; only the threshold
on $n$ depends on $N$. All posteriors can lie in $[1/4,3/4]$, and the
loss can be smooth, strictly proper, and bounded in $[0,1/2]$ throughout
the report interval $[0,1]$. Arbitrarily small-mass padding is not a
valid substitute for the anchors below.

\subsection{Notation inherited from the quartet}
For all sufficiently large integers $n$, write
\[
 d=(4\log n/n)^2,\quad b=d^2,\quad a=1/2-b,
 \quad (x_A,x_B,x_C,x_D)=(0,2d,1-2d,1),
\]
and let $\varphi_n''=g_n=\kappa_n(t)+\kappa_n(1-t)+d^2$ be exactly
the polynomial in Phase 9, with
\[
 p_n(t)=T_n\!\left(\frac{2t-1-d}{1-d}\right)^2,
 \quad Z_n=\int_0^1p_n(t)\,dt,
 \quad\kappa_n=p_n/Z_n.
\]
Its curvature has degree exactly $2n$ and is positive on all of
$\mathbb R$. Let $P=D(AB)=D(CD)$, $q=D(BC)$,
$T=D(ABC)=D(BCD)$, and $J=D(AD)$, calculated with the original
quartet masses $(a,b,b,a)$. The already-proved estimates are
\[
 \frac{bd}{2}\le P\le4bd,\qquad
 0<q\le bd^2,\qquad T\ge b/4,\qquad J\ge b/2.
\]
Every quartet pair other than $BC$ has cost at least $P$.

\subsection{Exterior growth supplies expensive anchors}
For $d\le1/4$, the maximum of $p_n$ on $[0,1]$ is $p_n(0)$:
on $[d,1]$ its value is at most one, while on $[0,d]$ the exterior
Chebyshev magnitude decreases towards one. Also
\[
 \operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac{1+d}{1-d}\right)
 \le\sqrt{\frac{4d}{1-d}}\le3\sqrt d.
\]
Consequently
\[
 Z_n\le p_n(0)\le \exp(6n\sqrt d)=n^{24}.
\]
For every $t\le-1/8$, the absolute value of the Chebyshev argument
is at least $5/4=\cosh(\log2)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_n(t)\ge L_n:=\frac{4^n}{4n^{24}}
 \qquad(t\le-1/8).
 \tag{A1}
\end{equation}
This elementary bound is deliberately loose; its exponential growth is
the only feature needed.

Fix $N\ge5$, let $m=N-4$, and put $\lambda=1/2$. Give the four
core states masses $\lambda(a,b,b,a)$. Add $m$ anchors with locations
and masses
\[
 z_j=-\frac12+\frac{j}{4(m+1)},\qquad
 p_{z_j}=\eta=\frac1{2m},\qquad 1\le j\le m.
\]
The augmented masses sum to one, all locations are distinct, and all
anchors lie strictly between $-1/2$ and $-1/4$.
Here $x$ is an auxiliary real coordinate, not yet the posterior
coordinate. All Jensen costs are well defined because $g_n>0$ on
$\mathbb R$; the affine lift below places the entire source inside the
legal posterior interval.
Write $\delta=1/[16(m+1)]$. If $u<v$ is any pair containing at least
one anchor, its left endpoint $u$ is an anchor, its gap is at least
$4\delta$, and the interval
\[
 E_{uv}=[u+\delta,u+2\delta]
\]
is contained in $[u,v]\cap(-\infty,-1/8]$. The pair kernel on this
interval satisfies
\[
 K_{uv}(t)=\min\{p_u(t-u),p_v(v-t)\}
 \ge\delta\min(p_u,p_v).
\]
For all sufficiently large $n$ depending on $m$, every source mass is
at least $\lambda b$. Combining this with (A1) gives
\begin{equation}
 D(uv)\ge\lambda b\delta^2L_n.
 \tag{A2}
\end{equation}
In particular, choose $n$ large enough that
\begin{equation}
 D(uv)\ge\lambda b/4
 \quad\hbox{for every pair containing an anchor}.
 \tag{A3}
\end{equation}
Indeed (A2) actually tends to infinity for each fixed $N$, since $b$
decays only polynomially in $n$. Thus (A3) remains available despite
the small masses of the quartet's two interior states.

\subsection{Every deterministic top-capacity hierarchy is expensive}
The feasible flat partitions retaining all anchors as singletons give
\begin{equation}
 0<\operatorname{OPT}_{N-1}\le\lambda q,
 \qquad
 0<\operatorname{OPT}_{N-2}\le2\lambda P\le8\lambda bd.
 \tag{A4}
\end{equation}
Positivity follows from strict convexity, positive weights, and distinct
locations: a partition with fewer than $N$ cells merges distinct states.

Any hierarchy using fewer than its allowed cells can be refined to
exactly $N-1$ fine cells and $N-2$ coarse cells without increasing
either distortion. To see this, first refine its fine partition within
the existing coarse cells to $N-1$ cells, and then split coarse cells
into unions of these fine cells until the coarse count is $N-2$.
Therefore it suffices to consider exact counts. The fine partition then
merges exactly one pair.

If its fine pair is not $BC$, its cost is at least $\lambda P$.
For core pairs this is inherited from the quartet. For pairs touching
an anchor it follows from (A3), $P\le4bd$, and $d\le1/16$.
Using (A4), its fine ratio is at least
\[
 \frac{\lambda P}{\operatorname{OPT}_{N-1}}
 \ge\frac Pq\ge\frac1{2d}.
\]

Otherwise, the fine pair is $BC$. An exact coarse partition merges
two of the $N-1$ fine blocks. If neither block contains an anchor, the
three possibilities are the original $ABC$, $BCD$, and $AD$ merges;
their resulting total distortion is at least $\lambda b/4$.
If an anchor is involved, the resulting coarse cell contains a pair
touching an anchor, so its cost is at least $\lambda b/4$ by (A3).
Here the required monotonicity is unconditional and uses the original
weights: for any $U\subset S$, the Jensen decomposition of $S$ into
$U$ and $S\setminus U$ gives $D(S)\ge D(U)$.
Thus the coarse ratio is at least
\[
 \frac{\lambda b/4}{\operatorname{OPT}_{N-2}}
 \ge\frac1{32d}.
\]
No contiguity restriction is used in this enumeration. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\varphi_n,\mathrm{det}}^{(N-2,N-1)}\ge\frac1{32d_n}.
 \tag{A5}
\end{equation}

The standard two-level random-function-table argument works for every
source size and every pair of capacities. From a stochastic hierarchy
of price $R$, sample its two independent channel tables. Conditional
Jensen bounds each expected deterministic distortion by the corresponding
stochastic distortion. The expected sum of the two normalized costs is
at most $2R$, so one sampled deterministic hierarchy has maximum
normalized cost at most $2R$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\varphi_n,\mathrm{stoch}}^{(N-2,N-1)}\ge\frac1{64d_n}.
 \tag{A6}
\end{equation}
For $N=4$, (A5)--(A6) are exactly the original construction.

\subsection{Interior support and globally bounded proper losses}
Use the common affine change
\[
 y=\frac38+\frac x4,
 \qquad x=4y-\frac32.
\]
All augmented source locations belong to $[1/4,5/8]\subset[1/4,3/4]$.
Define the finite positive number
\[
 M_n=\max_{[-3/2,5/2]}g_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\varphi_n(y)=
 \frac{\varphi_n(4y-3/2)}{16M_n}.
\]
Then $0<\widetilde\varphi_n''\le1$ on all of $[0,1]$, its curvature
still has degree exactly $2n$, and every augmented-source distortion is
multiplied by the same positive factor $1/(16M_n)$. Hence both prices
and (A5)--(A6) are unchanged. Positivity of the original polynomial on
the whole real line is essential to this step.

For clarity, bounded losses can be defined directly from the normalized
curvature $\widetilde g=\widetilde\varphi_n''$:
\[
 \ell(q,1)=\int_q^1(1-t)\widetilde g(t)\,dt,
 \qquad
 \ell(q,0)=\int_0^q t\widetilde g(t)\,dt.
\]
These are smooth and lie in $[0,1/2]$ for every report $q\in[0,1]$.
For true posterior $p$, the derivative of conditional risk in $q$ is
$(q-p)\widetilde g(q)$, proving strict propriety, and integrating this
identity proves that excess risk is $B_{\widetilde\varphi_n}(p,q)$.
This formulation does not require symmetry; in this particular lift
symmetry about $1/2$ is also preserved.

In degree-at-most-$r$ notation, take $n=\lfloor r/2\rfloor$. For every
fixed $N\ge4$, there is a threshold $r_0(N)$ such that, for all
$r\ge r_0(N)$, these witnesses have
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{det}}^{(N-2,N-1)}
 \ge\rho_{\mathrm{stoch}}^{(N-2,N-1)}
 \ge\frac1{9216}\frac{r^2}{(\log r)^2}.
\]
The coefficient is absolute; the degree threshold depends on $N$.
As in Phase 9, this is a multiplicative statement. Bounded-loss
normalization does not assert nonvanishing absolute excess cost.

\subsection{Why sufficiently tiny-mass padding can fail}
There is already a counterexample to naive padding for $N=5$.
Fix any one of the strictly convex four-state witnesses above, at a
fixed degree. Add a fifth, distinct state $z$ of mass $\varepsilon>0$
and scale all original masses by $1-\varepsilon$. Consider capacities
$(3,4)$, and let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Every original-core pair retains a strictly positive limiting cost,
whereas for a pair joining $z$ to a core state $i$,
\[
 D_\varepsilon(zi)
 =\varepsilon B_\varphi(z,x_i)+O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
Since $z$ is distinct and the generator strictly convex, the leading
constants are positive. Consequently the exact fine optimum eventually
merges $z$ with some core state $i_\varepsilon$, and its price can be
made exactly one by retaining that pair.

The three-cell flat optimum converges to the original quartet's
$\operatorname{OPT}_3=q>0$. This follows from continuity of the costs
and the finite number of partitions: restricting any three-cell partition
to the positive-mass core leaves at most three cells, and the original
$BC$ merge is feasible after attaching $z$ to a core cell.

There is always a compatible coarse partition that retains the original
$BC$ merge: if $i_\varepsilon\notin\{B,C\}$, merge $BC$ separately;
otherwise merge the fine block $zi_\varepsilon$ with the other middle
state. Its cost converges to $q$. The resulting hierarchy has fine ratio
one and coarse ratio tending to one. Hence
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{det}}^{(3,4)}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad \rho_{\mathrm{stoch}}^{(3,4)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
For each degree one can choose the padding mass small enough to keep
the new price below two, even though the original quartet price diverges
with degree. The fixed positive-mass exterior anchors avoid this failure
through the explicit pair-penalty estimate (A2).
