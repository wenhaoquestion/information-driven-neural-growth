\begin{abstract}
For $N\ge3$ distinct scalar posterior states with positive masses, we study
the hierarchy price at capacities $(N-2,N-1)$. For every twice
continuously differentiable strictly convex generator, either compatible
flat optima exist, or a restriction to four consecutive original states
has the same two flat costs and a deterministic hierarchy price at least
as large. The hierarchy class includes noncontiguous cells. Consequently,
for each fixed generator, the deterministic envelope over all finite
source counts equals the weighted quartet envelope. The inherited
polynomial quartet estimate gives the uniform upper bound
$1936r^2/(\log r)^2$ for curvature degree at most $r\ge2$.
An exterior-anchor construction embeds the inherited lower witnesses
into every prescribed $N\ge4$ and preserves their deterministic prices.
Both deterministic and stochastic degree envelopes therefore have order
$\Theta(r^2/(\log r)^2)$, with constants and a degree threshold independent
of $N$. The witnesses admit interior posterior support and smooth proper
losses bounded in $[0,1/2]$, while their absolute flat excess risks vanish.
Exact finite checks cover 300 instances and 67,860 compatible hierarchy
candidates. The result concerns only the last two capacities; arbitrary
prescribed capacities and simultaneous all-capacity hierarchies remain
outside its scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, contribution, and quantifiers}
An optimal representation with $k$ outputs need not be obtainable by
coarsening an optimal representation with $k+1$ outputs. For a scalar
posterior and a strictly proper binary loss, the excess Bayes risk of a
representation is a Jensen distortion. The hierarchy price measures the
smallest simultaneous multiplicative cost of requiring this coarsening.
We study the two capacities immediately below exact representation.

The preceding weighted four-state analysis~\cite{huang9} proved the sharp
degree order at capacities $(2,3)$, with no lower bounds on masses or
posterior gaps. The additional argument here is combinatorial: at
capacities $(N-2,N-1)$, the possible flat partitions leave only a
consecutive quartet as an obstruction to compatibility. Singleton
extension preserves both flat denominators and costs. For the lower
bound, expensive exterior anchors exclude every new merge, and an affine
change restores legal posterior coordinates without changing the price.

Let $C_{N,r}^{\detp}$ and $C_{N,r}^{\stp}$ be the suprema of the respective
prices over $N$ distinct locations in $[0,1]$, positive probabilities, and
generators whose nonnegative nonzero polynomial curvature has degree at
most $r$. The generator, weights, and locations may all vary with $N,r$.
The results below give an absolute $r_0$ such that, for every $N\ge4$
and every integer $r\ge r_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{9216}\frac{r^2}{(\log r)^2}
 \le C_{N,r}^{\stp}\le C_{N,r}^{\detp}
 \le1936\frac{r^2}{(\log r)^2}.
 \label{eq:sharp-envelope}
\end{equation}
For $N=3$, both prices are one. All logarithms are natural; $r$ is the
degree of the curvature, not of the generator.

Three quantifiers must be distinguished. The fixed-generator identity is
an equality between the supremum over all finite $N$ and the quartet
supremum. A general individual source is only dominated by a selected
quartet, and the inequality can be strict. The fixed-$N$ lower witnesses
are a separate construction in the polynomial degree class; they do not
give fixed-generator envelope equality for each $N$, or exact stochastic
price preservation.

\section{Interpretation, provenance, and remaining questions}
The bound is uniform in source count because the last two capacities
leave a deficit of only one and two cells. At general capacities a flat
optimum can have many nonsingleton cells; the exchange classification used
here no longer exhausts the possibilities. No claim is made about the
sharp degree order for arbitrary prescribed capacities, or about one
hierarchy that simultaneously approximates every capacity. For $N=4$
only, adding the one-output and four-output levels is trivial; this fact
does not extend the present theorem to general all-capacity hierarchies.

The centroid identity, scalar nearest-center contiguity, and Jensen
kernels are classical Bregman facts~\cite{banerjee}. The adjacent-triple
inequality and complete polynomial quartet estimate are inherited from
Phase~9~\cite{huang9}, while the endpoint lower construction originated in
the earlier working paper~\cite{huang8}. Appendices~\ref{sec:quartet-upper}
and~\ref{sec:ext-weights} reproduce the needed arguments, so no lower
kernel estimate or upper analytic dependency is left implicit. The new
claims of this stage are the arbitrary-$N$ quartet reduction, its envelope
consequences, and the uniform exterior-anchor embedding. This description
of project provenance is not a historical originality determination.

There is no minimum-mass or minimum-gap constraint in
\eqref{eq:sharp-envelope}; a joint constrained envelope remains a different
question. The bounded-loss normalization preserves ratios, and the
explicit absolute-risk bounds tend to zero. Large multiplicative prices
therefore give no nonvanishing absolute-risk separation. No neural
training, sample-complexity, optimization-efficiency, or generalization
claim is made. The internal review records and exact-arithmetic checks
are supporting evidence, not external peer review or formal verification.
