\subsection{Stochastic hierarchies and exact cardinalities}
For probability weights, a finite channel $X\to Z$ has distortion
\[
 D(Z)=\mathbb E\varphi(x_X)
       -\mathbb E\varphi\bigl(\mathbb E[x_X\mid Z]\bigr).
\]
A stochastic hierarchy is a degradation chain
$X\to Z_{N-1}\to Z_{N-2}$, with each output alphabet of size at most its
subscript. Its price $R_N^{\stp}$ uses the same two flat denominators.
Indeed, sample an independent deterministic encoder table $U$ realizing
any channel. Revealing the table and applying conditional Jensen gives
$D(Z)\ge\mathbb E_U D(Z\mid U)\ge\OPT_k$. A deterministic flat minimizer
attains the reverse inequality. Thus allowing stochastic flat encoders
does not lower $\OPT_k$. General positive weights are handled by common
normalization.

\begin{lemma}[Inherited two-level deterministic comparison]
\label{lem:ext-rounding}
For this finite-source problem,
\[
 \tfrac12 R_N\le R_N^{\stp}\le R_N.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given a stochastic degradation chain, independently sample deterministic
function tables for its encoder and coarsener, independently of $X$, and
denote the two tables together by $U$. Conditional on $U$ they define an
admissible deterministic hierarchy. Revealing $U$ decreases Jensen
distortion at each level, so $D(Z_k)\ge\mathbb E_U D(Z_k\mid U)$.
If the stochastic chain has price $R$, the expected sum of the two
conditional deterministic ratios is at most $2R$. Some table has sum,
and hence maximum ratio, at most $2R$. Taking the infimum over chains
gives $R_N\le2R_N^{\stp}$. Deterministic hierarchies are admissible
stochastic chains, proving the other inequality. The table is a proof
device, not a public random seed available to the predictor. This is the
Phase~9 comparison~\cite{huang9}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Filling both hierarchy budgets]
\label{lem:exact-budgets}
Any hierarchy with at most $N-1$ fine cells and at most $N-2$ coarse
cells can be refined to those exact cardinalities without increasing
either distortion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Split fine cells within their current coarse cells until the fine level
has $N-1$ cells. If the coarse level has fewer than $N-2$ cells, at least
one coarse cell contains two fine cells; split that coarse cell into
unions of fine cells. Repeat until its budget is filled. Jensen
decomposition makes each refinement nonincreasing in cost, and nesting
is maintained. No contiguous-cell condition is used.
\end{proof}
The strict-convexity assumptions make the relevant flat optima positive.
Repeated locations, zero weights, and identically zero curvature are not
included in the theorems; such extensions would require a convention for
zero-optimum ratios.
