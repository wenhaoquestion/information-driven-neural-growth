\section{Exact finite checks and a strict restriction example}
\label{sec:computation}
The finite checks use Python's exact rational arithmetic, with no
floating-point comparisons in the assertions. Their source and result
files are under \path{research/experiments/}. They test the deterministic
claim over a bounded grid; they do not prove a universal theorem, optimize
stochastic chains, establish an asymptotic order numerically, or train a
neural model.

The main check covers every combination of $N\in\{4,5,6,7,8\}$, three
location grids, four weight patterns, and five curvature polynomials:
300 instances. The curvatures are $f^2+1/1000$, where
\[
 f\in\{1,\ 1-2t,\ 1-6t+6t^2,\ 1-12t+30t^2-20t^3,
          \ 1-20t+90t^2-140t^3+70t^4\}.
\]
Thus degrees are $0,2,4,6,8$, and positivity is certified algebraically.
The location grids are uniform, quadratic, and a fixed four-point core
with additional ordered states. Weights are uniform, alternating $1$
and $1/100$, or have final four entries $(1,10^{-4},10^{-4},1)$ or
$(2,10^{-2},10^{-4},1)$ with preceding entries one.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-budgets}, it suffices to cover exact-cardinality
hierarchies. Each coarse partition has one triple or two disjoint pairs;
the fine pair lies within one coarse cell. There are
$\binom N3+3\binom N4$ coarse shapes and
$3\binom N3+6\binom N4$ compatible coarse/fine candidates per instance,
including every noncontiguous choice. The code minimizes the fine-pair
cost within each coarse shape before taking the outer minimum, thereby
covering all those compatible candidates without explicitly evaluating
each maximum separately.

For every optimal-choice combination, the assertions verify either a
compatible optimum or a consecutive quartet, equality of its two flat
costs, and domination of the global price. The independent restricted-
growth-string enumerator checks the specialized coarse-shape enumeration
for $N=4,5,6$; the analytical deficit classification justifies it for the
larger tested counts. All checks passed. The separate padding check uses
$\varepsilon\in\{1/10000,1/100,1/10,1/2,99/100\}$ and confirms the
formulas in Section~\ref{sec:padding}; its five samples are distinct from
the proof for the full interval $0<\varepsilon<1$.

\paragraph{Domination can be strict even when the global price exceeds one.}
The targeted diagnostic uses the independent weighted-variance formula
for $\varphi(x)=x^2/2$. With one-based indices, take
\[
 x=(0,1/36,1/9,1/4,4/9,25/36,1),\qquad
 p=(1,1,1,2,1/100,1/10000,1).
\]
Common probability normalization changes no price. The cheapest pair is
$\{5,6\}$; a coarse flat optimum uses $\{4,5\}$ and $\{6,7\}$. Thus the
selected quartet is $S=\{4,5,6,7\}$, with the exact common flat costs
\[
 q=\frac1{323200},\qquad Q=\frac{1004419}{5210440992}.
\]
Exact enumeration over all hierarchy shapes gives
\[
 R_7=\frac{10314341331}{10144631900}\approx1.016729
 <\frac{2073082097481}{2018982631900}\approx1.026795=R_4(S).
\]
The global optimum retains fine pair $\{5,6\}$ and adds pair $\{1,2\}$
at the coarse level. The quartet optimum instead merges $\{4,5,6\}$.
This example was found after 32 constant-curvature candidates in the
recorded search order. It directly rules out replacing the restriction
inequality by equality for every individual source.
