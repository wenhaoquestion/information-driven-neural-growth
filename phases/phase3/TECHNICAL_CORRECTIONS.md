# Historical technical corrections and negative results

The initial all-positive module target was constant and could be solved by an intercept. The final theorem uses even K and known alternating signs, making the all-useful target nonconstant. The invertible signed-label map preserves each paired-loss law, KL, waiting-time law and test constant. The superseded theorem, code and numerical summary remain under `research/unsigned_diagnostic/`; they are not the current theorem.

The reset lower bound restricts the entire observation interface to paired loss differences and assumes power conditional on the allowed past. Retained raw labels or other distinguishing training history can pay for a later test. The final text explicitly includes the history KL term and does not apply the restricted lower bound to the trained controller.

Early proposal random streams diverged across methods when operations consumed different random draws. Confirmation uses event-indexed candidate initialization. A weak sampled-pool/reset comparator was supplemented with persistent minibatch cycling and actual unique-label counts. Both adverse pilots remain archived, and the original pilot implementation is preserved in research/.

The EB linear floor causes many observed rejections of evaluator-positive proposals. This is gate conservatism, not evidence of minimax necessity. A separately frozen, fresh-seed betting follow-up improves acceptance; random proposals remain competitive and additional accepted capacity increases subsequent training work. The 64-run matched-final-width full-pool control was added post hoc and is labelled accordingly.

Movement and gain bound variance and characterize worst-case difficulty; they do not uniquely determine every pair's variance. Valid gates protect accepted checkpoints only, not ungated warmup/tails. The complementary-feature Gaussian-loss diagnostic is heuristic and pays for extra trials and large decoder coefficients. Lower-risk offline fixed models do not establish universal online or resource dominance.

These are historical technical findings, not transcripts of internal deliberation or external peer-review certification.
